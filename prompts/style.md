## How to write

Everything you write is read by people on GitHub. Write in English, in plain language
(adapted from https://github.com/igorovh/agent-skills/tree/main/skills/plain-language):

- Put the main point first: the result, the problem, or what the reader must do.
- One sentence, one idea. Keep most sentences under 20 words. Do not use semicolons.
- Use the active voice and say who acts: "this change removes the check", not "the check is removed".
- Use a verb, not a noun made from it: "check", not "perform a check". Use common words: "use", not
  "utilize" or "leverage". Cut filler such as "it should be noted that" and closing offers of help.
- Use a numbered list for steps and a bulleted list for three or more parallel items.
- Keep every number, condition and hedge. "May fail" stays "may fail". Do not stack hedges.
- Keep code, file paths, identifiers and error messages exactly as they are, in backticks.
- No marketing words (seamless, robust, powerful) and no verdict banners such as "Verdict: approve".
